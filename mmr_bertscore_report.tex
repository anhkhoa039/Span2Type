\begin{abstract}
This report evaluates the effect of \textbf{Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR)} entity sampling on LLM-based cluster naming in the OWNER system.
Cluster names generated by Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (via Ollama, $n{=}16$ entities) are scored with BERTScore (RoBERTa-large) against ground-truth CrossNER labels across five domains: AI, Literature, Music, Politics, and Science.
Results are compared against the OWNER baseline~\cite{cluster_naming_baseline}.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Main finding:} MMR and No-MMR produce nearly identical results for most domains (avg $F_1$: MMR~$= 0.923$, No-MMR~$= 0.926$, $\Delta = -0.002$).
No-MMR edges ahead in Music ($+0.011$) due to better entity-set coherence, while MMR performs slightly better in Literature ($+0.007$).
Both conditions underperform the baseline ($F_1 = 0.944$), primarily in domains where the clustering pipeline produces less cohesive clusters in these runs.
\end{abstract}

% ─── 1. Introduction ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

After clustering entity embeddings, OWNER assigns a human-readable name to each cluster using an LLM prompted with a small sample of entity strings.
A key design choice is \emph{how to select} those $n=16$ example entities.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{No-MMR (random) sampling}: entities are drawn uniformly at random from the cluster.
  \item \textbf{MMR sampling}: entities are selected to maximise both relevance (proximity to the cluster centroid) and diversity (dissimilarity to already-selected entities), producing a representative and non-redundant sample.
\end{itemize}

The hypothesis is that MMR yields a more informative prompt, helping the LLM infer a more accurate type label.
This report tests that hypothesis using BERTScore as the semantic similarity metric between the generated name and the ground-truth CrossNER label.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Music is the domain where No-MMR most clearly outperforms MMR ($+0.011$).
Two clusters drive this gap:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{C1} (\texttt{band}): MMR selects a diverse sample that includes album titles, leading the LLM to output \textit{albums} ($F_1 = 0.887$).  No-MMR samples more homogeneous band names, correctly yielding \textit{band} ($F_1 = 1.000$; $\Delta = -0.113$).
  \item \textbf{C18} (\texttt{event}): MMR's diversity introduces non-event entities, causing the LLM to respond \textit{multisport events} ($F_1 = 0.889$).  No-MMR keeps the sample event-focused and the LLM outputs \textit{event} ($F_1 = 1.000$; $\Delta = -0.111$).
\end{itemize}
In both cases, the cluster has a dominant entity type and MMR's diversity \emph{hurts} by sampling from the minority entity population.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
Science is the most challenging domain ($F_1 = 0.908$) and MMR/No-MMR tie on average.
The compound GT labels (\texttt{chemicalelement}, \texttt{chemicalcompound}, \texttt{astronomicalobject}, \texttt{academicjournal}) create a systematic penalty because the LLM generates natural English names (\textit{elements}, \textit{planet}, \textit{journal}) that share only partial token overlap with the concatenated label strings.
The largest individual discrepancy is C2 (\texttt{organisation}): MMR samples diverse institution types and the LLM outputs \textit{professional associations} ($F_1 = 0.885$), while No-MMR samples more homogeneous entries and outputs \textit{organization} ($F_1 = 0.983$; $\Delta = -0.098$).

\subsection{When MMR Helps vs.\ Hurts}

MMR improves naming when a cluster has \textbf{multiple valid entity sub-types} and diverse sampling ensures the LLM sees a representative cross-section (e.g.\ Literature C4: diverse geographic entities $\to$ \textit{country}).

MMR hurts naming when a cluster has a \textbf{dominant entity type} and diversity sampling introduces minority entities that mislead the LLM (e.g.\ Music C1: album titles mixed into a band cluster $\to$ \textit{albums} instead of \textit{band}).
This pattern accounts for both large negative deltas in Music (C1: $-0.113$, C18: $-0.111$).

% ─── 6. Conclusion ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{MMR has negligible overall impact on LLM naming} ($\Delta F_1 = -0.002$ across 92 clusters).  The choice between sampling strategies is secondary to cluster quality.
  \item \textbf{No-MMR is marginally preferred} overall ($F_1 = 0.926$ vs.\ $0.923$), driven by Music where high-purity clusters benefit from homogeneous (non-diverse) samples.
  \item \textbf{MMR is preferred when clusters are heterogeneous}: in Literature, diverse sampling helps the LLM identify the dominant entity concept rather than latching onto a frequent but non-representative sub-type.
  \item \textbf{Both conditions trail the baseline by $\approx 0.018$}, attributable to lower cluster cohesion in these experiment runs.  Improving cluster purity is the primary lever for improving naming quality.
\end{enumerate}
